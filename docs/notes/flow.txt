Yes — **almost**, but there is one important correction:

**We don't first generate random booking data and then apply the logistic model to clean it.**
The logistic model and the business factors are used **during the synthetic-data generation itself**.

Think of the whole project in these steps:

### Step 1 — Collect/reference real-world information

First define the things we know realistically:

```text
Routes
Distances
Bus types
Capacities
Typical fares
Route popularity
Weekdays
Holidays
```

This becomes your **reference data**.

---

### Step 2 — Generate the basic booking curve

For every trip, generate the expected booking pattern using the **logistic/S-curve**.

```text
Days before departure
        ↓
Logistic function
        ↓
Base expected bookings
```

Example:

```text
20 days → 3 seats
15 days → 8 seats
10 days → 17 seats
5 days  → 29 seats
0 days  → 37 seats
```

This gives you the **basic demand pattern**.

---

### Step 3 — Apply factors affecting demand

Now modify that base curve based on things like:

```text
Route popularity
Price
Holiday
Weekend
Bus type
```

For example:

```text
Base bookings = 20

Popular route
20 × 1.20

Holiday
24 × 1.15

Higher price
↓ demand

Noise
± small random variation
```

So now you get **realistic synthetic bookings**.

---

### Step 4 — Apply cancellations

Near departure, simulate some cancellations.

```text
Before cancellation = 35
Cancellations = 2

Final bookings = 33
```

Also enforce:

```text
Final bookings ≤ Bus capacity
```

---

### Step 5 — NOW you have your raw synthetic booking data

For example:

```text
T001 | Mumbai-Pune | 10 Aug | 20 days | 3 seats
T001 | Mumbai-Pune | 11 Aug | 19 days | 4 seats
T001 | Mumbai-Pune | 12 Aug | 18 days | 5 seats
...
```

This is essentially your **raw booking event data**.

---

### Step 6 — Store raw data in Bronze

You keep the raw/generated booking events.

```text
Bronze
   ↓
Raw booking data
```

Don't do heavy transformations here.

---

### Step 7 — Clean and transform → Silver

Now you clean/validate the data:

* correct data types
* remove invalid records
* check duplicate records
* check bookings ≤ capacity
* check valid trip IDs
* calculate occupancy %

For example:

[
Occupancy% =
\frac{Bookings}{Capacity}\times100
]

Now you have:

```text
trip_id
booking_date
days_left
seats_booked
capacity
occupancy_pct
```

This becomes your **Silver daily table**.

---

### Step 8 — Create the two final tables

Now you can create your two useful datasets.

### `bus_booking_daily`

**One row = one trip on one day**

```text
T001 | 10 Aug | 20 days | 3 seats | 7.5%
T001 | 11 Aug | 19 days | 4 seats | 10%
T001 | 12 Aug | 18 days | 5 seats | 12.5%
...
```

### `bus_trips_master`

**One row = one complete trip**

You aggregate the daily data:

```text
T001 | Mumbai-Pune | capacity 40 | price 600 | final bookings 37 | final occupancy 92.5%
```

So yes, **the two tables come after the generation + cleaning/transformation process.**

---

# Your complete flow

```text
                  REAL-WORLD REFERENCE DATA
                  / Route / Fare / Capacity /
                  / Bus Type / Holiday etc. /
                            ↓
                     GENERATE TRIPS
                            ↓
                  LOGISTIC BOOKING CURVE
                            ↓
                  APPLY DEMAND FACTORS
              / price / route / holiday /
              / weekend / bus type /
                            ↓
                    ADD CANCELLATIONS
                            ↓
                       ADD NOISE
                            ↓
                RAW SYNTHETIC BOOKINGS
                            ↓
                         BRONZE
                            ↓
                 CLEAN + VALIDATE DATA
                            ↓
                         SILVER
                            ↓
               ┌────────────┴────────────┐
               ↓                         ↓
      DAILY BOOKING TABLE          MASTER TRIP TABLE
      1 row = trip + day           1 row = trip
               ↓                         ↓
               └────────────┬────────────┘
                            ↓
                     FEATURE ENGINEERING
                            ↓
                           GOLD
                            ↓
                       ML TRAINING
                            ↓
                    PREDICT FUTURE
                   BOOKING TRAJECTORY
                            ↓
                       DASHBOARD
```

### One very important distinction

**Logistic curve is not your ML model.**

At the beginning, you're using the logistic function to **generate realistic synthetic historical data**.

Later, you train an actual ML model on that generated historical data:

```text
Synthetic generation:
Logistic + factors + noise
             ↓
Historical dataset
             ↓
       ML model learns
             ↓
Future booking prediction
```

That's the architecture you should keep in your head.
